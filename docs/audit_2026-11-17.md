# Audit of the custom engine, 2026-11-17

Synthesis of six read-only audits (search, training, inputs-and-heads, self-play regime, rules-and-arena,
evaluation-and-opponent) and a skeptic's pass that re-read every cited line. 50 findings were raised; 47 hold as
stated, 3 hold only in part (section 5). Every `file:line` below was confirmed by the skeptic against the tree on
branch `custom-engine` (submodule `third_party/hivemind` at v2.2.2-39). Nothing here was measured by running the
engine; the numbers quoted are from existing logs (`runs/custom/*`, the build reports) and pure arithmetic on the
code's constants.

The one-paragraph verdict: **no result produced so far says anything about the stated goal**, and **a first PACE
training round run on the code as it stands would very probably learn nothing again**, for reasons that are now
specific and fixable. The headline opponent (upstream aminwoo/hivemind v3 twin-s-clock) has never been played; every
gate compares a clock/k contender against stock it04 at a fixed 100 nodes, at 60 s, with the engine charged for fewer
nodes than it searches, at a simulated speed that is the Mac CPU's. The tree's model of time is lockstep: the opponent
is assumed to reply at exactly our pace, a pass costs one macro-ply, and five hand-written terminal rules disagree with
the arena. Self-play cannot generate the data that would teach clock use: no exploration, the pass action is never
expanded at play budgets, all games start at equal clocks, a seat that keeps moving cannot flag, and the one sample
that would show "the clock at which this net stopped sitting" is the one the dedupe deletes. The training recipe then
fine-tunes all 13.6M it04 weights at the from-scratch rate on that data, with no promotion rule.

## 1. Summary: the biggest problems, in plain words

### 1.1 The measurements do not measure the goal

1. **The target engine is not in the arena.** Every row in `docs/beat_stock.md` and every `iterate.sh` gate plays stock
   it04 (`cluster/iterate.sh:179-182`, `arena/pools/phase1.json`). Our UCI driver speaks the fork's dialect
   (`position ... clocks`, `TimeAdvantage`, `FreePass`, `TreeMoveMs`), not upstream's (`TimeModel clock`,
   `TimeLeadMs`, `TeamMoveTimeMs`; theory 06 §2.2). Upstream reports +156..+290 self-relative Elo over the flag model.
   Beating it04 is a milestone, not the goal, and nothing in the repo compares the two starting points.
2. **The simulated speed is the Mac CPU's.** `MS_PER_NODE = 5.5` (`arena/players/hivemind.py:92-94`) is applied to
   every binary and every network, so a "60 s game" is ~110 moves at ~100 nodes. On the hardware either engine really
   plays on, 550 ms is tens of thousands of nodes. The 25-200 node regime where `runs/beat` found "faster always wins,
   no B*" is policy-prior-dominated and is not where the best engine plays.
3. **Small budgets get free search.** `go nodes N` overshoots by about one batch per thread (100 -> ~146, 25 -> ~70;
   `hivemind.py:33-35`) and only the requested N is charged (`hivemind.py:689-692, 715-717, 767, 798`), although the
   real count is parsed (`hivemind.py:117`). A 25-node arm gets 2.8x the search per charged second of a 200-node arm.
   The "faster fixed budget wins all the way down" slope and the single positive 180 s row (`long_clock110_v300`
   0.652) are both confounded by this.
4. **The iterate gate compares two different experiments.** Contender: clock/k with k ~ U(60, 300), jittered
   0.5-2.0x; opponent: fixed 100 nodes, "placeholder" (`iterate.sh:47, 52, 54`). Neither compute nor time is equal,
   there is no it00 row (unchanged widened net under the same k range), so a rising score cannot be attributed to
   training. Everything is at 60 s (`iterate.sh:50`), where the human-scale sits the project is about do not exist.
5. **The statistics are weaker than printed.** The gate opening set is 8 chess openings = 56 ordered pairs
   (`arena/openings.py:82-102`), so the cluster bootstrap has 56 clusters, not 800; `compare_evals.py:38` uses a
   binomial +-; one `same_*` control sits on the acceptance boundary (0.535); 180 s rows are capped at 600 games
   because an 8 h job writes `summary.json` only at the end (`arena/pool.py:328`, `cluster/eval.sbatch:16-17`).

### 1.2 The tree's model of time is wrong in a way RL cannot fix

6. **Lockstep macro-plies.** One `make_moves` charges `treeMoveMs` to the on-turn seat of each board, move or pass
   (`board.cc:508-513`); children strictly alternate teams (`node.h:429, 564`); `TreeMoveMs` is our own jittered
   think time (`hivemind.py:767, 773`). So the opponent is modelled as replying in exactly our time, a seat with 3 s
   makes 15 moves at 200 ms in the tree, a one-sided "sit for the knight" needs 2k macro-plies to see k partner moves,
   and a pass is under-priced by at least the 500 ms `free_sit_ms` (`hivemind.py:802-804`). This is the same class of
   error as the lockstep problem found earlier, one level down.
7. **Five hand-written terminals disagree with the arena.** (a) Mate races: the root team is assumed to lose every
   simultaneous mate race, from either side (`searchthread.cc:85-88, 200-203`; `agent.cc:717-757`), so V_root(A) and
   V_root(B) can both be "loss". (b) Double flag in one macro-ply is a draw in the tree (`searchthread.cc:144-150`)
   but a loss for the lower clock in the arena (`state.py:70-81`), and on an exact tie the arena picks the loser by
   seat name (`A_black` first). (c) Twofold repetition and rule50 are draws in the tree (`board.h:669-677`) and do
   not exist in the arena (`RULES.md:38`), so a perpetual on A zeroes a won B; every search also starts from a bare
   FEN (`hivemind.py:142-151`) so planes 35-36 are always 0 in arena games. (d) D1 sit-off: the team 1 ms behind
   loses the game (`board.h:153-166`) instead of merely having to move. (e) `TimeAdvantage` plane 31 and its hash bit
   are frozen at the root value through the whole tree (`searchthread.cc:497, 585-589, 620-621`) while planes 37-40
   move with the tree clocks, so a long sit line feeds the net contradictory clocks, and the one time input it04 has
   actually learned points the wrong way exactly where it matters.
8. **The pass action is invisible to the search at play budgets.** Progressive widening allows 13/16/20 children at
   50/100/200 visits (`node.h:345-368`, `search_params.h:713-715`), candidates come in joint-prior order
   (`joint_action.h:201-291`), FPU penalises unvisited edges by up to 1.0 (`node.cc:442-446`), Dirichlet is 0 and
   Gumbel is off (`search_params.h:722, 773-774`). With a pass prior of 0.05 the (pass, best) pair first expands at
   ~82 visits; at 0.01, at ~7,400. The net must already want to sit for a sit to get a visit.

### 1.3 Self-play cannot produce the data the clock net needs

9. **No exploration anywhere.** Free-mode searches send only `FreePass`, `MateProbeAnyTime`, `TreeMoveMs`
   (`hivemind.py:773`); `OpeningNoise` is never set (`uci.cc:638`), there is no temperature option, the played move is
   a deterministic argmax (`agent.cc:6568-6575`). Stock's own `selfplay.cc` has Dirichlet 0.3/0.25, temperature
   decay and raw-policy openings (`selfplay.h:14-29`); none of it reaches the arena path. Openings are 240 ordered
   pairs of 16 chess lines (`openings.py:90-104`), no bare start position.
10. **Policy targets are the prior.** Recorded searches are 36-182 nodes at move 1 and hit the 16-node floor under
    ~16 s (`hivemind.py:675-680`, `selfplay.py:213`); the target is the root visit distribution with weight 1
    regardless of node count (`training_sample.cc:109-134`, `trainer_agent.py:554`). At those budgets without noise
    the target is a sharpened copy of it04's prior.
11. **The clock regime teaches the wrong lessons.** All four clocks start equal (`state.py:37`, `selfplay.py:90`),
    so the only time asymmetry is the per-team k draw, which ties "behind on time" to "searched deeper"
    (`hivemind.py:658, 675-680`). A seat that keeps moving cannot flag (floor 44 ms/move, no latency floor: 156-454
    moves to flag from 60 s); every recorded flag came from sitting. Jitter is a unimodal multiplier on latency only.
    Base time is welded to 60 s and to node count through `ms_per_node`.
12. **The dedupe deletes the decisive sample.** Repeats are keyed on FENs only (`hivemind.py:768-770`), so after
    "pass at t0" every later search in the same position, including the one where the net stops sitting and moves, is
    unrecorded. The clock planes are the point of the widened net and the only samples where only the clocks vary are
    the ones removed (59% of searches in v2smoke).
13. **Half the GPU is spent on sit-off tails.** A decided sit-off runs to the flag: ~40 s of mutual sitting per 60 s
    game, 59% of searches are fresh-tree re-searches of an unchanged position that also block the partner's machine
    (`hivemind.py:758-761, 815-817`; `sim.py:397`). A real sitter costs his partner nothing.

### 1.4 The training recipe is likely to damage it04 before the clock planes help

14. **Whole trunk at the from-scratch rate, no promotion.** `rl_train_config` max_lr 0.0025 NAG, one parameter group
    (`train_config.py:216`, `trainer_agent.py:895-897`), fresh cosine cycle every 2,000-game iteration
    (`train_loop.py:493-497`), batch silently 512 -> 256 without lr rescale; `iterate.sh:76-83` always continues from
    the previous weights and the gate "is a measurement, not a filter" (`iterate.sh:16-17`). Precedents for transfer
    use 10-35x lower trunk rates. Meanwhile the zero-init clock columns see inputs of 0.003-0.2 and grew to only 3.8e-3
    in the smoke run.
15. **The decisive number is not an input.** Planes 37-40 are our seat and the same-board opponent at `min(t/300,1)`
    and `exp(-t/10)` (`planes.cc:216-237`). The diagonal t_ourA - t_oppB is reachable only as a linear combination of
    two 1/300-slope planes (a 10 s lead = 0.033), and training planes are stored uint8 (`training_sample.cc:94-104`),
    so clocks land on a 1.18 s grid and the exp planes are exactly 0 above 62 s. The exact clocks sit unused in the
    sidecar.
16. **Value labels are noisy and the aux heads mis-scaled.** Flags count as +-1 like mates with `q_value_ratio` 0
    (`recorder.py:234-238`, `convert_selfplay_data.py:79`), in games whose flag is decided by an unobserved jitter
    draw; the `arrivals` head regresses raw 5 s counts with MSE (`trainer_agent.py:980-988`) with no end-of-game mask;
    `think_gain`'s `0.5 + 1.5p` multiplier cuts mean think time by about a third when the head is calibrated
    (`hivemind.py:748-750`) and its labels sit near the search-noise floor.

## 2. Decisions the user must make

### D1. Starting network: widened it04 (`hivemind-clock-v2`) vs upstream twin-s-clock

Options:
- (a) **Keep widened it04** (the default in `iterate.sh:81`). Pro: richer clock model already built on it (absolute
  clocks, elapsed, FreePass tree, heads), 13.6M params may win at tens of thousands of nodes. Con: upstream's 0.97M
  twin-s distilled net already beat it04 at fixed 100 ms through 3x nps (theory 06 §2.2); the first hundreds of
  GPU-hours may only climb to where upstream stands.
- (b) **Start from upstream's promoted twin-s-clock weights**, porting our planes 37-43, FreePass tree and heads to
  `TwinBoardNet`. Pro: starts at the target's level with clock planes already trained through 13 iterations; MIT
  licence allows it. Con: 5-8 engineering days, a different plane layout (upstream feeds lead and speed ratio, not
  absolute clocks), and our engine's plane writer and trainer both change.
- (c) **Distil it04-clock into a small net** before RL (theory item 17). Only if the headline is at equal wall-clock.

**Recommendation: do not decide yet; make the decision from one measurement.** Run three arms at equal wall-clock on
the gate GPU, 60 s and 180 s, 1,600 games each: it04 stock, widened it04 unchanged (must read 0.50 vs it04, which
doubles as the parity gate), upstream twin-s-clock latest (~40 GPU-hours, after WP-A and WP-G). Rule: gap under ~50 Elo
-> (a); gap over ~100 Elo at realistic budgets -> (b); in between, (a) for the first round with (b) prepared. Why: the
direction is genuinely uncertain (13.6M nets may dominate at 20k nodes/move, where no one has measured), the cost of
measuring is under 1% of the budget, and guessing wrong costs 500+ GPU-hours either way.

### D2. Headline metric and time controls

Options for the metric:
- (a) **Equal nodes.** Reproducible, cheap, measures net quality; ignores speed, and the whole upstream result says
  speed decides at equal time.
- (b) **Equal wall-clock on identical hardware, modelled as nodes-as-time with a per-arm `ms_per_node`** measured on
  the gate GPU for each binary+network. Reproducible and contention-proof like (a), and it is what "beats the best
  engine" means.
- (c) Real wall-clock. Only for the final match (cluster contention leaks into results).

**Recommendation: (b) as the headline, with one equal-nodes diagnostic row per iteration** to separate net quality
from time management, and (c) once at the end. Write it into `docs/beat_stock.md` before any new row is run.

Time controls: **gate at 120 s and 180 s** (two rows per iteration, or one 180 s row with 60 s demoted to a smoke).
Self-play samples base time from {60, 120, 180, 300} s (weights ~0.25/0.25/0.35/0.15), increment 0. Why: the
10-30 s sits humans describe exist only at 180 s and up, the only positive signal so far was at 180 s, and bughouse is
played at 2+0 / 3+0. The cost is real (long games 3-5x) and is why WP-C decouples node budget from base time and WP-B
adjudicates sit-offs.

### D3. Opponent set

**Recommendation, in order of what each proves:**
1. `upstream-v3` twin-s-clock latest under its own time manager: the target. Gate against it from iteration 1.
2. `it00` = widened net with zero clock weights, same k range, same jitter, same seeds: the baseline row every
   iteration's score is reported against (score minus it00 score, with the cluster-bootstrap CI).
3. Stock it04 at a budget spending the same total clock as the contender's mean: continuity with `beat_stock.md`.
4. Fairy-Stockfish bughouse (XBoard, CPU): the external floor; reproduce the paper's ">95% at 2 s" once with stock.
5. `same_*` controls, three more seeds, pooled spread published as the noise floor.

### D4. Arena and tree rule profile (must be one rule in both places)

Recommendation: double flag on an exact tie = draw; FICS-style repetition (board only, counter reset by any drop,
threefold) = draw in the arena, and in the tree a repetition is non-terminal under tree clocks (or scored by the
clocks, never 0 while the other board is winning); rule50 off under tree clocks; `min_move_ms` 100 plus an additive
lag draw; flag immediate (keep a `claim` profile as a later option); `max_plies` kept as a safety abort, scaled by
base time, and an aborted game scored by the on-turn clocks or dropped from training; stalemate = wait (documented).
D1 sit-off becomes "the lower seat must move" once the durative tree exists; stays as fallback under constant
`TreeMoveMs`.

### D5. Does the durative tree (spec v3) land before the first training round?

Options: (a) run round 1 on the lockstep tree with the cheap fixes only; (b) land per-seat ready times first (1-2
weeks C++). **Recommendation: (b), run in parallel with everything else, and allow one throwaway pipeline round
without it whose data is not kept.** Why: findings 6-8 are the structural reason sitting cannot be learned from the
tree; a round trained on lockstep data is a round that must be discarded, and 1-2 weeks is cheap against 10k GPU-hours.

### D6. Which heads to keep on in round 1

Recommendation: `think_head` off (fix the multiplier and labels later, or replace with the return-trained gate after
the clock net trains); `arrivals` on only after the log1p/Poisson, per-type weights and end-of-game mask land, and
ablated in one round; `movesLeft` discount set to 0 for the clock build until the label definition is unified.

## 5. Findings that did not hold, or held only in part

- **Search 1, "tree reuse and the permanent brain are silently dead in the clock build":** the code reading is right
  (raw clocks in the key, retained candidates hashed on predicted clocks), but the arena sends `ucinewgame` before
  every search by default (`hivemind.py:191, 281-282`), so reuse is off for stock and clock alike in every arena game;
  there is no clock-vs-stock asymmetry in the gate and nothing to A/B. Downgraded to a latent bug that must be fixed
  before `reset_tree=False` is used anywhere (WP-E E1, WP-C sitter re-decision).
- **Rules 1, "the 400-ply cap ends most non-mate games as a draw":** every recorded engine game ended by flag or
  checkmate (smoke, v2smoke, local1h: 0 of 14 by `max_plies`); the 102/200 figure is from the C++ stub random player.
  The "cannot flag by moving" half is real and belongs to `min_move_ms` and actual-node charging (WP-B, WP-C); the cap
  stays as a safety abort with corrected scoring.
- **Rules 4, "stock returns `bestmove (none)` and the arena plays a heuristic move for it":** wrong mechanism. In the
  stock path `_parse` sets both moves to None and the controller returns a wait for both seats (`hivemind.py:300-304,
  712-727`); the heuristic fallback only fires on an arena-illegal move. The real, different flaw: when stock judges one
  board stuck it stops moving on its healthy board too, a resignation-by-sitting no server imposes. Count it and decide
  (WP-C).
- **Inputs 8, partial:** the fp16 network is indeed not parity-checked on value/policy, but `cluster/check_clock_engine.py`
  already checks `think_gain` on the TensorRT build (extend it, WP-F), and the 43-channel `planes_parity.py --clocks`
  run was completed in v2 (0/405 positions, 0/86 channels, top-1 800/800); that item is dropped.
- **Inputs 6, partial:** "~13 arriving pieces per sample" is from 20 s test games; the two recorded 60 s games averaged
  5.6. The loss-balance concern is smaller at 60 s but the structural fix (log1p/Poisson, per-type weights, mask) stands.
- **Search 9, captured-piece latency:** holds, but the auditor's own impact estimate (low) is the right one; both sides
  transfer at landing, and the error is only that the delay is tied to our think time. Folded into the durative tree
  and ablated, not built on its own.
